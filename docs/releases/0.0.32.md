# GemPlayer 0.0.32

A test build of GemPlayer, a media player for Ubuntu Touch modelled on VLC
for Android. The video side of the app is whole: a video player, and around
it a library that can be sorted, filtered, grouped and played as a queue.
So is the audio side: a music library by artist, album, track, genre and
file, an audio player with a mini-player, and a queue that plays on in the
background and is kept from one launch to the next. The Browse tab goes
through the phone's folders, and the Playlists tab keeps lists of your
own, a stream plays from its address once streams are allowed, and what
was played is kept in a history, there are settings, and one search looks
through the whole library. Files and links come in from other apps and go
out to them. This build is about files that cannot be played: it says so
in plain words, and a queue moves on past them.

**If you have 0.0.26 or earlier, update.** Up to 0.0.26, a song played
right after a video could show its own title and play the video's sound:
it happened when the song was a single one, or the first of its album or
list. And in 0.0.13 to 0.0.17, opening the app after the queue had moved
on in the background started the current track again, and closing the app
could leave the music playing.

Tested on one device only: a Pixel 3a running Ubuntu Touch 24.04-1.x.
What this build adds, and the Content Hub, search, settings, history,
streams and playlists of the six builds before, were checked on it from a
computer, over adb; they have not been tried by touch yet. A real
hand-over from another app could not be started that way at all: the
app's side was tried with the same addresses the system hands over, and
the system lists GemPlayer as it should. The settings' switches for the
gestures could not be tried from the computer either.

## New since 0.0.31

- A file that cannot be played says so in plain words: that it is not there
  any more, or that it could not be played and why that may be. Before, a
  missing video showed the system's own technical message, and a missing
  or broken song said nothing for 20 seconds.
- A file on the phone is given 8 seconds to start, where it was given 20;
  a stream still has 20.
- In a queue, an item that cannot be played is skipped after a moment, and
  the next one plays, as in VLC. A queue where nothing plays stops trying,
  even with "repeat all" on.
- After a failure the play button shows "play", and the audio player's
  message has a dark box behind it.

## Network

GemPlayer does nothing over the network except play a stream whose address
you give it, and that is switched off until you allow it. With streams off,
which is how the app arrives, it makes no connection of any kind.

With streams allowed, the address you open is handed to the system's media
player, which connects to it; GemPlayer has no code that connects to
anything. The addresses you open, and the history of what you played, are
kept on the phone, in the app's own database, and go nowhere else. The app has no network shares, no casting, no
remote control, and looks nothing up online.

## What it does

- Lists the music on the phone by artist, album, track, genre and file;
  sorts and filters those lists, can show only your favourite tracks, and
  plays from any of them. A track's menu leads to its album, its artist and
  a page of information about it.
- Lists the videos on the phone, as a grid or a list, and plays H.264, HEVC,
  VP9 and AV1.
- Sorts the list by name, file name, length or how recently a file was
  added, filters it as you type, groups it by name or by folder, and can show
  only your favourites.
- Lets you make groups of your own, rename them and dissolve them.
- Plays a folder, a group, a selection or the whole list in order, and adds
  to what is playing as audio: next, or at the end.
- VLC's gestures: tap for the controls, double tap to skip 10 s or pause,
  swipe sideways to seek, swipe up and down for volume (right half) and
  brightness (left half), pinch to fill the screen.
- **Play as audio**, and back to video. Audio carries on with the screen off
  and under other apps, its queue moving on by itself, and behind the lists
  with a mini-player above the tabs.
- An audio player with the video player's gestures on its cover: double tap
  and swipe sideways to seek, swipe up and down for volume and brightness.
- Shuffle, and repeat of the whole queue or of one item; a queue page with
  a filter, where an item still to come can be moved or removed, and
  playback told to stop after a chosen track.
- Brings back, when it is opened, the queue that was playing as audio.
- Resumes each video where it was left.
- Sleep timer, jump to time, A-B repeat and bookmarks, for video and for
  audio; a screenshot of a video.
- Shows a `.srt` subtitle file that sits beside the video, with a delay
  control.
- Twelve picture sizes, a lock, and its own rotation, whatever the system's
  rotation lock says.
- Opens files and links handed over by other apps, several at once as a
  queue, and shares a video or a song with another app.
- Goes through the phone's folders in the Browse tab, plays a file or a
  whole folder from there, and keeps your favourite folders at hand.
- Keeps playlists of music and videos: filled from the menus of the other
  tabs or from the play queue, played whole or from any item, and put in
  order by hand.
- Plays a stream from its address, once you have allowed streams, and
  keeps a history of the addresses.
- Keeps a history of what was played, the latest 200 items, which can be
  cleared and switched off.
- Has settings for resume, the gestures, the skip length, the look of
  subtitles, and what is kept.
- Searches the whole library at once: videos, artists, albums, tracks,
  genres and playlists.
- VLC's five tabs along the bottom.

## Before you install

- **The app is unconfined.** It runs outside Ubuntu Touch's sandbox, because
  the system does not let a sandboxed third-party player open the files in
  Music and Videos. It reads media files and writes only its own settings,
  with one exception that you have to ask for: "Keep running in the
  background" adds the app's name to a list in the system's settings, and
  takes it out again when switched off. `docs/STORE.md` has the details.
- The package also contains two development aids: a diagnostics page (More →
  Diagnostics) and a remote control that stays off unless a marker file is
  created by hand. Both are removed before 0.1.0.

## Known limits

- No playback speed, no choice of audio track, no embedded subtitles. The
  system's playback service does not offer them.
- A seek lands on the keyframe before the place asked for, so seeking,
  resume, bookmarks and A-B repeat can start a few seconds early.
- The sleep timer and A-B repeat run only while the app is on screen. With
  the screen off the timer fires late, when the app is next opened. "Keep
  running in the background", under Settings, changes that.
- With "Keep running in the background" on, a video is paused three
  seconds after the app is left, not at once; and it is also paused if the
  system's panel is held open that long. If the app is removed while the
  switch is on, its name stays in the system's list, where it does nothing.
- The search is under More; the magnifying glass on the Video and Audio
  tabs is still each list's own filter. It finds a track by its title, and
  an artist or album by its name; it does not look through the stream
  history or the folders of the Browse tab. A section lists the first 20
  of what it found.
- The settings have one skip length for audio and video, and no choice to
  be asked before a video resumes. A seek in a video lands on the keyframe
  before its target, so a skip there can come out shorter than set.
- A queue of several videos makes a break of about a second when it is
  switched to audio or back to the picture: the system can move through a
  list by itself only without a picture, so the video is loaded again.
- Videos can be added to the queue only while it plays as audio. Items that
  have played, and the one playing, cannot be removed from it or moved;
  items still to come are moved from their menu, not by dragging.
- Opening the app after the queue moved on in the background makes a short
  break in the music, while the app finds its place again.
- If the app is ended by the system instead of being closed, its music can
  play on to the end of its queue. Playing anything stops it.
- Only tracks can be favourites, not artists, albums or genres.
- An artist's tracks cannot be listed all together any more, only album by
  album; the play button on the artist's page plays them all.
- An album whose files are spread over two folders shows as two albums.
- A cover is shown only when it is inside the music file itself. Nothing is
  looked up online.
- The Browse tab shows no hidden files, and a folder plays its music or its
  videos, not both together. Removable storage is built but has not been
  tried with a card or a stick.
- A playlist holds an item once. Its items are moved from their menu, not
  by dragging. A folder adds its own files to a playlist, not those of its
  subfolders. The video player has no "Save playlist".
- A playlist with music in it plays as audio, a video among it too; "Play
  as video" in the audio player shows the picture. A playlist of videos
  only plays with its pictures.
- A file that was deleted or moved stays in its playlists and does not
  play.
- In the Audio tab's selection bar, the plus now asks whether to add to
  the play queue or to a playlist.
- A stream is opened as a video unless its address ends like a music
  file; one without a picture moves to the audio player a moment after it
  starts. A live stream shows a length of 0:00.
- When the system gives no reason, a missing file cannot be told from a
  damaged one, and the message names both. A file that needs more than 8
  seconds to start, if there is such a one on a slow card, would be called
  failed.
- An address that does not answer is given 20 seconds before the player
  says so. An address typed without `http://` or `https://` is taken for
  `https`.
- Switching streams off while one plays pauses it and closes its queue.
  The system's player keeps the paused stream loaded until something else
  is played.
- The history is on when the app is first installed or updated. It lists
  each item once, at the time it was last played. Clearing it leaves
  resume points, played marks and the stream history as they are.
- "Share" is in the menu of one video or one track, not yet for a
  selection, a group or an album. The other app gets a copy of the file.
- Files and a link handed over together: the files play, the link is left.
- The system's media controls show the playing file under the name "Media
  Player".
- A clip shorter than about 20 s never shows a progress bar: its first 10 s
  count as not started and its last 10 s as finished.
- "Recently added" goes by the date a file was last changed; the system's
  library does not record when a file arrived.
- Groups of your own exist only under "Group by name"; with "Group by
  folder" or no grouping they are kept but not shown.
- The information page shows a video's resolution as a class (1080p, SD),
  not in pixels, and lists no tracks or codecs: the system reports neither
  reliably.

## Install

```bash
adb push gemplayer.yenis_0.0.32_all.click /home/phablet/
adb shell "gdbus call --system --dest com.lomiri.click --object-path /com/lomiri/click --method com.lomiri.click.Install /home/phablet/gemplayer.yenis_0.0.32_all.click"
```

The phone needs Developer Mode on. It installs over an earlier build and
keeps its resume points, bookmarks, favourites, groups and playlists. Streams stay as you had them; on a new install they are off until you allow them. `docs/INSTALL.md`
has the full steps, and how to remove the app again.
